Human-readable report of the paper-v2 work. Updated at the end of every task.

## 1. Status

Every Must task is DONE. T3.7 (hosted large VLMs) was not run because it needs paid calls (G-SPEND, default no), and the optional T4.6 (federated simulation) was not started, so federated learning stays future work. Every evaluation script writes its numbers, with run id, seeds, date, git hash and library versions, into `results/manifest.json`, and `tools/check_numbers.py` checks every quoted number against it (624 claims). Work is on branch `paper-v2`; the starting commit 2521134 is tagged `baseline-pre-v2`, and its outputs are kept in `results/baseline_20261023/`. The main findings: the LSTM plus Isolation Forest hybrid does not beat Isolation Forest alone on F1; unsupervised anomaly scores cannot separate unusual from malicious behaviour, while a supervised layer trained on labelled incidents, using the same six features, can (on our 10 datasets and on CERT r4.2), but only for attack types and environments it was trained on; about 10 to 20 labelled incidents are enough for a simple logistic layer; gated fusion beats the multiplicative SBRS at an equal alert budget; the PII cascade saves no VLM calls at equal accuracy; and the best local VLM (gemma-3-4b-it) stays well below the hosted 72B model's ceiling on harder and real documents. The paper's primary claim has changed as a result; it is stated below. Section 4 lists what went against the design.

### Primary claim (replaces "the hybrid LSTM plus Isolation Forest engine detects threats")

> Unsupervised anomaly detection, including our LSTM plus Isolation Forest hybrid, cannot tell unusual behaviour from malicious behaviour, and it misses almost all slow insider drift. The same six behavioural features, given to a supervised layer trained on a deployment's own analyst-confirmed incidents, separate the attack types it has seen, and about 10 to 20 confirmed incidents are enough. The supervised layer does not detect attack types it has not seen and does not transfer to another environment, so it complements the unsupervised detectors and the drift detector rather than replacing them. We therefore propose a layered design: unsupervised detection and CUSUM drift detection always on, plus a supervised layer trained on the deployment's confirmed alerts, with slow drift given its own alert budget.

Supporting contributions: the measured on-device PII track (PaddleOCR against EasyOCR, three local VLMs, the cascade's failed cost rationale), the CUSUM drift detector as the label-free option for slow drift, and one-command reproducibility with every threshold chosen on validation.

Limits the paper must state with this claim: all behavioural data are synthetic (our generator and CERT); the supervised layer fails on attack types it has not been trained on and does not transfer from the simulator to CERT (N-071, N-072); the supervised layer learns from attacks produced by the same simulator it is tested on (N-067); a new deployment has no labels on its first day, and 1 to 5 labels make the layer worse than Isolation Forest (N-068); gradient boosting is unstable below about 50 labels (N-069); CERT has 7 test insiders.

Suggested title: "From Anomalous to Malicious: Label-Efficient Behavioural Risk Scoring for SaaS Data-Loss Prevention, with a Reproducible Evaluation". This is a suggestion for the writing assistant; the title is the authors' decision.

## 4. Results that went against the design

The full list, with evidence keys, is `results/negative_results.md` (N-001 to N-073). The most important:

- The supervised layer only knows what it was shown: it misses every slow-drift episode when trained without slow-drift examples, and it scores below chance on CERT when trained on the simulator (N-071, N-072).

- Unsupervised anomaly scoring is the weak point, not the features: supervised models on the same features beat every unsupervised detector on all 10 datasets and on CERT, and richer features make Isolation Forest worse (N-061, N-062, N-065, N-066).
- The supervised layer needs about 10 labelled incidents to beat Isolation Forest; 1 to 5 make it worse, and gradient boosting is unstable below about 50 (N-068, N-069).
- A shared 1 percent alert budget is consumed by loud attacks, so slow drift is missed entirely even by a fully trained model (N-070).

- The hybrid does not beat Isolation Forest alone on F1 (difference 0.003, CI -0.013 to 0.020), and its AUC is significantly below the LSTM alone, a per-user z-score and max fusion (N-018 to N-024).
- Slow insider drift is nearly invisible to the hybrid (episode recall 0.117 under the comparison rule, 0.133 at the deployed threshold). CUSUM raises it to 0.567 but fires on 20 percent of benign-burst hours (N-014, N-029, N-030).
- The multiplicative SBRS escalates 1 of 35 low-sensitivity, high-anomaly threats; the calibrated beta ranges from 2.0 to 8.0 across datasets (N-017, N-033, N-034).
- At attack intensity 0.25 no detector separates slow drift (AUC about 0.5) (N-026).
- On CERT r4.2 the hybrid reaches F1 0.057 and AUC 0.733, and validation chose alpha 0.00, which drops the LSTM (N-051 to N-056).
- Substituting EasyOCR for PaddleOCR cost accuracy (v2 F1 0.516 against 0.701), and PaddleOCR is about 5 times slower on CPU (N-041, N-042).
- The cascade saves no VLM calls at equal accuracy: the calib-chosen tau and the learned router both send almost every image to the VLM (N-049, N-050).
- Local VLMs: Qwen2.5-VL-3B is far below every other path; Qwen2.5-VL-7B is below gemma-3-4b-it on every set and barely fits 6 GB; gemma reaches only 0.407 on real FUNSD forms (N-046 to N-048, N-057 to N-060).
- An image upload is held for seconds in Tier 2, so the original "300 ms end to end" goal cannot hold for images (N-039).

### Must not make

- That the LSTM + Isolation Forest hybrid outperforms its components overall. It does not beat IF on F1 and is below the LSTM, the z-score and max fusion on AUC (N-018 to N-024).
- That gated fusion escalating all 35 low-sensitivity, high-anomaly threats is evidence for it: the gate escalates every session with A >= tau_A, and those 35 threats are defined by A >= tau_A, so 35 of 35 holds by construction. Report the multiplicative form's 1 of 35 as its failure (N-033) and the recall difference at equal budget as the gated form's gain.
- That the multiplicative SBRS is the best fusion, or that low content sensitivity safely suppresses alerts (N-033, N-034).
- That benign bursts are suppressed on every dataset (one dataset: 14.8 percent, N-016).
- Any single-dataset number (seed 42) as the headline; use the 10-dataset mean with its interval.
- The old draft figures: EWMA FPR 42.7 percent vs hybrid 11.2 percent, SBRS F1 0.93, PII coverage 0.91, 279 ms end to end.
- "Real-time 300 ms end to end". Replace with the measured Tier 1 budget (T3.6).
- That PaliGemma-3B was evaluated (it was not), or that any local VLM matches the hosted Qwen2.5-VL-72B (the 72B ran only on the old v1 corpus).
- That a bigger local model is better: Qwen2.5-VL-7B is below gemma-3-4b-it on every corpus (N-057).
- That the cascade saves cost by sending only hard images to the VLM (N-049, N-050).
- That the system performs well on CERT, or that CERT is real enterprise data (it is synthetic, and the feature mapping is ours; N-051, N-055, N-056).
- That episode recall alone shows skill on CERT (N-053).
- That richer features or a bigger unsupervised model would fix CERT detection (N-061, N-062), or that the supervised probe is deployable without labelled incidents (N-064).
- That the supervised scores (AUC 0.951, 0.987 on CERT) are what a deployment would see: the probes learn from the same simulator's attacks, and a new deployment has no labels (N-067).
- That the supervised layer detects new attack types or transfers between environments (N-071, N-072), or that it can replace the unsupervised detectors (N-073).
- That a few labelled incidents suffice: 1 to 5 make the supervised layer worse than Isolation Forest (N-068). Also not that slow drift is caught at any alert budget: at 1 percent, below the attack share, it is missed entirely (N-070).
- That federated learning, differential privacy or a usability study was done.
- That "existing DLP ignores behaviour" (Microsoft Purview Adaptive Protection uses insider-risk levels in DLP).
- That OCR confidence never ranks difficulty: on v2 it does (AUC 0.884); on v1 it did not.
- That Tier 1 meets its budget for every payload or under load: 64 KB of text takes about 5 s, and 16 concurrent clients push p99 to 845 ms (N-077, N-078).
- That the data are realistic enterprise traffic or real handwriting. All data are synthetic; handwriting is font-rendered.
- The value beta = 2.5 as a general constant: the validation knee ranges from 2.0 to 8.0 across datasets (N-017).

## 6. New or remaining limitations

- All behavioural data and all PII documents are synthetic (Faker, font-rendered handwriting). FUNSD adds 199 real scanned forms with partial gold (D-021). CERT r4.2 adds a second synthetic benchmark, where the engine scores far lower (hybrid F1 0.057, AUC 0.733, N-051); it has only 7 test insiders, and its results depend on our feature mapping (D-024).
- Each test split holds about 6 malicious-insider episodes, so per-dataset episode recall moves in steps of 1/6; the 10-dataset mean and the cluster bootstrap carry this uncertainty.
- The attack generator's "slow drift" spans about 12 hours, not days.
- The CUSUM drift detector fires on 20 percent of benign-burst hours.
- The street-address recogniser has low precision (0.35).
- LSTM training uses CPU torch; GPU training was not used (D-003).
- The LOF neighbour grid was widened after looking at seed-42 validation and test AUC (D-008, disclosed).
- Network bandwidth on this laptop (50 to 300 KB/s to Hugging Face and figshare) limited which local VLMs could be downloaded (D-012). Three were run: Qwen2.5-VL-3B, gemma-3-4b-it and Qwen2.5-VL-7B, all at Q4_K_M with context 4096; quantisation and prompt were not varied.
- Hosted large VLMs were not rerun on the new corpora (G-SPEND), so the v1 ceiling result (Qwen2.5-VL-72B, 240 of 240) has no v2 or FUNSD counterpart.
- FUNSD gold is derived and partial (D-021), so FUNSD precision is a lower bound.
- PaddleOCR and the VLMs were timed on this laptop only (PaddleOCR on CPU; D-017). Timings are not portable.
- The deployed tau_ocr (0.95) and the deployed fusion parameters come from one calibration split or dataset (seed 42); the 10-dataset spread is in the claims files.
- Federated learning, differential privacy and a usability study were not built or run.

## 8. How to reproduce everything

```powershell
pwsh -NoProfile -File .\reproduce.ps1            # tests, anomaly track, ablation, drift, fusion, statistics, sweep, claims check; about 20 min

CERT r4.2 is not in the script because it needs a 4.8 GB download; its four commands are in the T2.6 row. `python -m tools.build_paper_inputs` rebuilds `paper_inputs/` from the manifest, and `python -m tools.render_figures` rebuilds Figure 1.
