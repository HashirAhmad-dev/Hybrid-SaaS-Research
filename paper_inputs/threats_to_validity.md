# Threats to validity (input for the paper rewrite)

Each point names the evidence. Numbers are means over the 10 generated datasets unless stated.

## Construct validity

- **Synthetic behaviour.** All 50 users, their sessions and every attack are generated by `evaluation/generate_sessions.py`. The attack signatures were written by us. Three of the four attack classes reach AUC near 1.0 at the default intensity, which says more about how loud the injected signatures are than about the detectors. The intensity sweep (T2.3) shows the same detectors at AUC 0.72 to 0.74 when the signatures are scaled to a quarter of their strength.
- **Slow drift is short.** A "malicious insider" episode spans 6 to 10 active hours (about 12 hours of wall time). Real insider drift can last weeks. The drift detector's time to detection (median 8.9 hours) must be read against this short horizon.
- **Synthetic documents and font handwriting.** The main PII corpus is generated with Faker and rendered with system fonts; "handwritten" means a handwriting typeface, not real handwriting. Real scans enter only through 199 FUNSD forms (old business forms, not modern enterprise uploads). FUNSD has no PII labels, so we derived a partial gold list from labelled form fields (D-021). PII outside those fields is not in the gold, which makes FUNSD precision and F1 lower bounds.
- **Content sensitivity S.** In the behaviour data S is derived from the file's class label plus noise, not from running the PII pipeline on real files. The fusion study (T2.5) therefore tests how S and A are combined, not how well S is measured.

## Internal validity

- **Selection on validation only.** Every threshold, weight, band, drift parameter and router is chosen by `evaluation/selection.py`, which refuses test rows (unit-tested). One disclosed exception in spirit: the LOF neighbour grid was widened after looking at seed-42 validation and test AUC (D-008).
- **Statistical dependence.** Windows inside one episode and sessions of one user are correlated. Intervals use a two-stage cluster bootstrap (users, then episodes), not window-level Wilson intervals, which are kept only as a secondary column.
- **Small episode counts.** Each test split holds about 6 malicious-insider episodes, so per-dataset episode recall moves in steps of one sixth.
- **Timing measurements.** Latency was measured on one laptop (i7-11370H, CPU). OCR timings were partly taken while other jobs ran (D-015) and are upper estimates. PaddleOCR ran without oneDNN (D-017).

## External validity

- **No real enterprise traffic and no external benchmark.** The CERT insider-threat dataset could not be obtained (every usable release is over 4 GB and the link to figshare ran at about 1 KB/s, D-011). The results are a statement about this simulator.
- **One model family per component.** One LSTM architecture, one forest, two OCR engines and small quantised local VLMs (Qwen2.5-VL-3B and gemma-3-4B at Q4_K_M in LM Studio, context 4096). Results for a VLM depend on its quantisation, image resizing by the serving software and the prompt; none of these was varied. Hosted large models were not rerun on the new corpus (no spending, G-SPEND).
- **Commercial context.** Microsoft Purview already applies DLP and insider-risk policies to text found in images (OCR, pay per image) and feeds behaviour-based risk levels into DLP (Adaptive Protection). Our contribution is not the combination itself but an open, auditable design with measured trade-offs and negative results (`docs/REFERENCES_VERIFIED.md`, section E).

## Conclusion validity

- The headline comparisons that are significant under the cluster bootstrap: hybrid F1 above LSTM, z-score, LOF and EWMA; hybrid AUC below LSTM, z-score and max fusion. The hybrid versus Isolation Forest F1 difference is not significant. The paper must not state or imply that the hybrid beats its components overall.
